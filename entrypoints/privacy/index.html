peer

UPDATED NOVEMBER 11, 2026

Privacy policy

peer runs in your browser. It has no account system, backend, analytics, or telemetry. The extension does not send your browsing activity to its developer.

What peer processes

To detect known tracking services, peer compares accessible HTTP(S) request addresses with its bundled catalog. Request paths are processed in memory. Request bodies, cookie values, and full page URLs are not saved in the activity history.

Temporary activity for each open tab includes the site hostname, timestamps, endpoint hostnames, request identifiers for deduplication, and company counts. It is kept in browser session storage and removed when the tab closes or the browser session ends.

What stays on your device

Your blocking and link-cleanup choices, language, eyes setting, and site exceptions are stored locally. An endpoint exception stores the website hostname and the destination you allowed. Optional history and the eyes indicator are off by default. When enabled, history stores visited site hostnames and daily company counts for the last seven calendar days, including today. Disabling history stops new entries; use “Delete history” in Settings to remove existing history.

History is not recorded for incognito tabs. Expired entries are removed when peer processes or displays data. Browser session data and persistent history are separate.

Permissions

Access to HTTP(S) sites and the webRequest API lets peer recognize known tracking requests and place its optional eyes widget on pages. Declarative Net Request blocks matching requests. activeTab allows peer to inspect available blocking results after you open it from the toolbar. Storage keeps settings and local data.

Link cleanup

When Peer back is on, peer removes known tracking parameters from links you follow. It skips signed links and common login and payment paths. URLs are processed in your browser.

Limits

The bundled lists include EasyPrivacy network rules and exceptions, specific endpoints, and known tracking aliases from AdGuard. Filtering uses the lists stored in the extension. Detection is not proof of the data a company received. peer does not reveal advertising profiles, change your IP address, prevent every kind of fingerprinting, or detect all first-party and server-side tracking.

External links

Links to GitHub and service documentation open only when you choose them. Those websites have their own privacy practices. Brand assets are bundled. Chrome and Edge retrieve site icons from the browser. Firefox uses a generic site icon. peer does not send visited site addresses to a third-party favicon service.

Contact

For questions or reports, use the project’s GitHub issues. Avoid including private browsing information.
